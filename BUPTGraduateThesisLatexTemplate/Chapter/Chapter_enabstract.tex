\begin{enabstract}
With the rapid development of low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite communication systems, the application scope of LEO satellite terminals continues to expand and their deployment scale keeps increasing. While these systems improve wide-area connectivity and communication flexibility, they also bring new challenges to radio security supervision. Especially in complex urban environments, non-cooperative LEO satellite terminals may exploit building blockage and complicated propagation conditions to conduct concealed access or unauthorized radiation, making their fast detection and accurate localization a key problem in radio security supervision. Compared with conventional fixed monitoring stations, low-cost unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) platforms provide higher mobility, more flexible deployment, and adjustable observation perspectives, and therefore offer a new technical approach for terminal investigation in dense urban areas. However, under the combined influence of urban multipath, non-line-of-sight propagation, local blockage, and the limitations of platform payload and onboard computing capability, traditional geometric localization methods and static fusion strategies often fail to achieve a satisfactory balance among localization accuracy, environmental adaptability, and engineering practicability. To address these issues, this thesis studies the localization and identification of non-cooperative LEO satellite terminals based on low-cost UAV platforms from two aspects: single-target continuous localization and multi-target concurrent localization.

In the single-target scenario, the reliability of different observations is jointly affected by the propagation environment and motion state and varies over time, making it difficult for fixed-weight or static fusion rules to adapt stably to complex scenarios. To address this issue, a physics-aware deep reinforcement learning based adaptive fusion localization method is proposed. Here, physics-aware means that physical prior information characterizing multipath complexity, observation fluctuation, and geometric consistency is explicitly introduced into the fusion decision state space. First, a weighted fusion model of heterogeneous localization information is established, and the dynamic weight adjustment problem is formulated as a Markov decision process. Then, based on ray-tracing scene modeling, physical prior features including root mean square (RMS) delay spread, received signal strength indicator (RSSI) variance, and geometric consistency are extracted to construct a physics-aware state space. On this basis, a Physics-Aware Deep Q-Network, namely PA-DQN, is designed. By introducing an incremental action space and a reward function that jointly considers short-term accuracy and long-term smoothness, the proposed method realizes dynamic and adaptive adjustment of fusion weights for multi-source observations. Experimental results show that, on the overall test set, the proposed method achieves a root mean square error (RMSE) of 2.15 m and a 95th-percentile error (P95) of 3.87 m, and outperforms RL-IFA and EKF in both overall localization accuracy and tail-error control. In addition, under low signal-to-noise ratio conditions, the localization error of the proposed method increases more slowly as the signal-to-noise ratio decreases, while the output trajectories retain better local stability and smoothness.

For the multi-target concurrent localization problem, where sparse spatial scanning, local multi-peak responses, and multipath ghosts make traditional peak-based decision methods unreliable, a robust cooperative localization method based on spatial signal representation is further proposed. First, a propagation field model for complex urban environments is constructed based on ray tracing, and high-fidelity spatial response simulation is implemented with OpenStreetMap (OSM) based scene modeling and a MATLAB toolchain. Then, through RSSI grid mapping and Radio Map reconstruction, discrete spatial observations are transformed into radio heat maps with regional structure, thereby forming a spatial signal representation for region-level discrimination. Furthermore, considering the random node dropout that may occur in multi-UAV cooperative networks, a teacher-student knowledge distillation framework is designed. The teacher model trained with complete observations is used to supervise the student model under incomplete observations, so that the student network can still approximate the decision capability of the complete network in the presence of missing nodes. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively alleviates performance degradation caused by random node dropout and maintains relatively stable localization performance under medium and high dropout ratios, which confirms the effectiveness of knowledge distillation for robust cooperative localization in complex environments.

In summary, this thesis establishes a localization and identification framework for non-cooperative LEO satellite terminals based on low-cost UAV platforms. The results demonstrate that integrating physical priors, deep reinforcement learning, and knowledge distillation significantly enhances localization accuracy and robustness in complex environments. This work provides valuable references for radio security supervision in complex scenarios.

\end{enabstract}
